UPI Track – Privacy Policy

Last updated: 9 October 2026

UPI Track is a personal money tracker for India. It reads the SMS your bank sends about payments, and statements you import, and shows where your money went. There is no server behind the app and no account with us: everything is processed on your own device.

What the app reads

  • Bank SMS (Android, with your permission; on iPhone through a Shortcuts automation you set up). Only messages from bank senders are used; OTPs, promotions and personal messages are ignored.
  • Statements you choose to import (PDF or CSV). They are read on the device and never uploaded by the app.

Where your data is stored

Transactions, categories and settings are stored in a database on your phone, or in your browser when you use the web app. Uninstalling the app, or clearing the browser's site data, removes that copy.

Optional sync with Google Drive

If you turn on sync, the app signs you in to Google and asks for one permission: drive.appdata, access to a hidden folder in your own Google Drive that only this app can see. Before anything is written there it is encrypted on your device with a key derived from a passphrase you choose. Google stores only encrypted files plus a small plain file holding the salt and a key check. Neither Google nor the developer can read your transactions. Your Google account name and e-mail are used only to sign you in and are not stored by the app anywhere else.

Sign out to forget the key on a device, use Reset sync to delete the Drive files, or revoke the app's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

What the developer receives

Nothing automatically. The app has no analytics, no advertising and no crash reporting. The only data that ever leaves your device by the app's own action is the encrypted sync data going to your Google Drive, and the report a message feature, which sends an anonymised copy of a bank SMS the app could not read only when you choose to send it, after amounts, names and numbers have been masked.

Update check

On Android the app asks GitHub once a day whether a newer release exists. That request carries no personal data.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.
